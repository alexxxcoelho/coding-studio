# Analyse: from codes to themes {#analyser}

## Why this stage {#analyser-pourquoi}

This is where thematic analysis happens. Braun and Clarke (2006, 2021) stress that themes do not "emerge" from the data: the researcher constructs them from the codes, in light of their questions. A theme is therefore not a topic ("costs") but an analytical proposition ("adoption depends on the match between the effort required and the value received"), which can be qualified or contradicted, and that is what makes it verifiable. The second half of the stage applies the framework method of Gale et al. (2013): a matrix whose rows are cases and whose columns are themes or codes, to compare interviews without losing the link to the passages. The stage is iterative: a theme points back to its codes, a passage to its interview.

## What you do {#analyser-actions}

### 1. Group the codes {#analyser-regrouper}

Before formulating themes, check that your codes are grouped. A focused code is a parent that brings together neighbouring descriptive codes, for example "No need" over "Paper loans judged sufficient" and "Few loans per day".

**In Coding Studio.** In the Codebook, drag the descriptive codes under a parent (see [Code](02_code.md#coder-codebook)). For each group, open the passages of the parent and check that you can say in one sentence what links them. The tool calculates no grouping on your behalf.

### 2. Create a theme {#analyser-themes}

A theme groups codes and carries an interpretation. It is built after coding.

**In Coding Studio.** Click "Analyse" (Analyser) in the top bar. The "Themes" (Thèmes) column lists your themes, with a "New theme" (Nouveau thème) button. For each theme, the list shows the title, the status, the supporting codes and the number of interviews concerned. Click "New theme", then enter a title that asserts something. Fictional example: "Non-adoption is not always a failure: some small libraries identify no need that the new loan system would meet." Then drag codes into the theme. A code can serve two themes. The title of a theme is editable; the old one remains in the history.

### 3. Fill in the theme record {#analyser-fiche}

The record is the defence file for the theme.

**In Coding Studio.** Click a theme to open its "Theme record" (Fiche de thème). It contains: the proposition in two to four sentences; the research questions concerned (Q1, Q2, ...); the supporting codes; the supporting cases; the divergent cases; the conditions (for example, a small volume of loans); the alternative interpretation; the limits; the status; and the decision memo. Cases and passages are linked from the matrix (action 6) or from exploration. Fill in the proposition and the divergent cases first: a theme with no examined divergent case is suspect.

### 4. Set the status and run the checks {#analyser-statut}

A theme goes through three statuses: candidate, revised, retained. "Retained" means you are ready to defend it.

**In Coding Studio.** In the record, the "Status" (Statut) selector offers "Candidate" (Candidat), "Revised" (Révisé) and "Retained" (Retenu). The "Checks" (Contrôles) button before "retained" (study list, 7 by default) opens a tick list. By default:

1. Are the extracts consistent with one another?
2. Is the theme distinct from the others?
3. Does the label assert something, rather than naming a domain?
4. Is the theme central to at least one research question?
5. Does it rest on several observations, or on a single case justified as revealing?
6. Which data qualify or contradict it, and are they dealt with?
7. Do the quotations retained suffice to support exactly the scope of the claim?

The list is edited in "Settings" (Réglages): you can add, remove or reword items. The decision memo, at the bottom of the record, explains in a few lines why the theme is kept. The checks are a decision aid, not a lock: the tool never moves a theme to "retained" on your behalf.

### 5. Read the themes through a framework, if you have chosen one {#analyser-cadre}

The interpretive framework is optional. If it has been chosen in Settings, it appears here and in "Write" (Rédiger), nowhere else.

**In Coding Studio.** In a theme's record, the "Reading through the framework" (Lecture par le cadre) selector shows the dimensions of the chosen framework (for TOE: T, O, E) and an "outside the framework" box. Tick what the theme explains and, in the memo, what escapes it. A theme may partly fall outside the framework: that is information, not a defect. Without a framework, this selector does not appear. The framework appears only here and in "Write".

### 6. Set the matrix {#analyser-matrice}

The matrix compares interviews across several themes or codes. It is set according to the question you are asking.

**In Coding Studio.** Below the themes, the "Matrix" (Matrice) tab offers two selectors. Rows: the interviews, the cases, or an interview attribute (for example library type). Columns: themes, focused codes, or custom dimensions that you defined in Settings (for example "who imposes the system", "who funds", "decision and situation"). Each combination is a setting of the same view: a case-by-theme matrix, a library-type-by-cost-supported matrix, and a research-question-by-extracts matrix are three settings, not three tools.

### 7. Read a cell {#analyser-cellules}

Each cell summarises what an interview says on a column, without hiding its source.

**In Coding Studio.** Click a cell: the "Cell" (Cellule) panel (interview, column) opens below the matrix, with the relevant passages, their quotations and the linked memos. There you choose the value from a menu: "present" (présent), "not mentioned" (non évoqué), "contradicted" (contredit) or "uncertain" (incertain), and you can add a one-line summary ("the batch is enough"). The number of passages is calculated by the tool. An interview not yet coded shows "not yet coded" (pas encore codé). "Not mentioned" does not mean absent in reality: the subject did not come up in the interview. The counters always distinguish passages from interviews: seven passages in two interviews do not make seven respondents.

### 8. Filter to bring out contrasts {#analyser-filtres}

Contrasts between groups are often more instructive than averages.

**In Coding Studio.** Above the matrix, the filter bar offers the attributes entered in the interview records (see [Prepare](01_prepare.md#preparer-attributs)). For example, filter on the status of statements "lived deployment" to compare those who lived through the change, or on the type "school" to see only that group. The matrix recalculates. Filters change no coding.

### 9. Explore the data {#analyser-explorer}

Exploration is used to test an intuition or to find what you may have missed.

**In Coding Studio.** The "Explore" (Explorer) tab offers four tools: keyword search in context (KWIC) across all interviews, with the neighbouring words of each occurrence; passages by code, across all interviews; passages nearby by vocabulary (see [Code](02_code.md#coder-lexique)); and the codes-by-attribute table, which breaks down the number of passages of each code by an attribute value. These tools explore; they do not conclude. Clicking a result opens the passage in its interview.

### 10. Document the decisions {#analyser-decisions}

Some studies observe concrete decisions. The tab that gathers them serves as illustration.

**In Coding Studio.** The "Documented decisions" (Décisions documentées) tab lists the decisions created. Click "New decision" (Nouvelle décision): give it a name (for example "Municipality X library, new loan system"), the outcome (adopt, defer or refuse) and the characteristics useful for your study, then attach units from any interview. A decision with no unit attached is marked "not documented". This tab complements the thematic analysis without replacing it.

## Common pitfalls {#analyser-pieges}

- Naming a theme after a topic ("Costs"). If the title is not a sentence that could be contradicted, it is not a theme.
- Taking frequency for importance. A rare mechanism can shed light on a condition; a repeated word may explain nothing.
- Reading silence as absence. "Not mentioned" only says the subject did not come up.
- Retaining a theme without an examined divergent case, or without an alternative interpretation.
- Forcing the framework: if everything fits neatly into it, re-read, because the framework may be getting in the way. A part "outside the framework" is normal.

## What this stage delivers {#analyser-sortie}

- A limited set of themes, each with a proposition, supporting cases, divergent cases, limits and a status.
- Decision memos that say why each theme is kept or dropped.
- One or more annotated matrices, filterable by attribute.
- Possibly, a reading through the chosen framework and a list of documented decisions.
- What is needed to move on to writing: each retained theme points back to its passages.

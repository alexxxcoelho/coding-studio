# INT-01, cleaned and diarised transcript (fictional)

Key: INT = interviewer, RESP = respondent. The company, the people and the figures are invented for this example.

---

**INT `[00:01]`:** Thank you for taking the time. Could you start by describing your role and the stores you run?

**RESP `[00:06]`:** I manage operations for a regional outdoor supply chain with three stores and one distribution centre. I am responsible for receiving, stock counts and the relationship with our logistics provider.

**INT `[00:25]`:** When did you first look at item-level identification?

**RESP `[00:29]`:** Our largest supplier started tagging cartons at source two years ago. They told us that from a given date it would be a condition of the next contract, so we had to decide.

**INT `[00:52]`:** What was the main reason you went ahead?

**RESP `[00:56]`:** Stock accuracy. Our counts were off by about thirty percent on some lines. Customers would order online, the system said the item was on the shelf, and it was not. Reading the tags at the dock let us see what arrived without opening every carton.

**INT `[01:30]`:** What did the pilot actually show?

**RESP `[01:34]`:** After six months the accuracy on tagged lines went from around seventy percent to about ninety-six. That was the number that convinced the board. We measured it with blind counts that we ran ourselves, so I would not call it an independent audit.

**INT `[02:05]`:** And the costs?

**RESP `[02:08]`:** The tags are cheap, but the readers at the dock and the handheld devices were not. Our finance team wanted a payback under two years, and we only reached that because the supplier shares part of the tag cost.

**INT `[02:40]`:** Who in the company had to change how they worked?

**RESP `[02:44]`:** The receiving team. They used to scan barcodes one carton at a time. Now they walk through a gate and the system reads everything. The first weeks were chaotic because nobody knew which reader zone to stand in.

**INT `[03:15]`:** Did you have the skills in-house to run this?

**RESP `[03:19]`:** Not for the integration. Our ERP was installed ten years ago, and the vendor had to write an interface to take the tag events. We had one person who understood both the warehouse and the database. When he left in the middle of the project we lost months.

**INT `[03:58]`:** How did you handle that?

**RESP `[04:01]`:** We hired a consultant for the interface and kept a support contract with the vendor. It cost more than we planned. The integration was the hardest part, not the hardware.

**INT `[04:30]`:** Was there any pressure from outside the company, beyond the supplier?

**RESP `[04:35]`:** Two of our larger customers, the ones who buy for projects, asked whether their orders could be traced to the item. It was not in their contracts yet. Sales people told us they expected it within a few years, and that influenced the board.

**INT `[05:10]`:** Were there concerns about the tags themselves?

**RESP `[05:13]`:** Some staff asked whether tags could be read from outside the building, and whether customers could read them after purchase. We deactivated the tags at the till, and we wrote a short note for staff explaining what the tags contain. No customer raised it with us.

**INT `[05:55]`:** Looking back, what would you do differently?

**RESP `[06:00]`:** I would involve the receiving team from the first day, not after the purchase. I would also separate the pilot from the business case. The pilot numbers were good, but they came from a small range of products that are easy to tag.

**INT `[06:40]`:** Do you think a smaller company could do this?

**RESP `[06:45]`:** Only if the supplier does the tagging. A store with ten suppliers and none that tag at source would have to tag its own stock, and the labour cost would kill the case. That is my honest view.

**INT `[07:20]`:** Is there anything you expect from your suppliers in the next years?

**RESP `[07:25]`:** Consistency. Some suppliers tag the cartons, others only the pallets, and our readers do not handle both well. We need a shared standard for what is on the label and where it sits, and I think the sector will have to agree on one.

**INT `[08:00]`:** Thank you. Is there anything you would like to add?

**RESP `[08:04]`:** Only that the numbers I gave you come from our own counts over six months. Please treat them as one company's experience.
